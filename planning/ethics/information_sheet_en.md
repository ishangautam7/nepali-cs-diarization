## What is this study about?

We study how well automatic speech technology can tell speakers apart in a recording — who spoke when — when people talk naturally in a mix of Nepali and English. Your recording helps us test and improve that technology.

There is one more specific detail about our aim that we will share with you at the end of the session. We hold it back because knowing it in advance could change the way you talk. It does not affect you or your recording in any way. You can ask about it, and withdraw, after we explain it.

## What will happen?

- You and 1–2 other people talk freely for about 45–60 minutes about everyday topics.
- Each of you wears a small clip-on (lavalier) microphone. One more microphone records the whole room.
- We record audio only. There is no video.
- At the end we explain the extra detail about our aim, and you may withdraw.
- You are paid [AMOUNT] for taking part, whether or not you withdraw. You may take part in at most 2 sessions; payment is per session.

You choose what to talk about. There is no test and no right answers. Taking part brings you no direct benefit.

The other people in the session will hear what you say, so please treat the conversation as private and do not repeat it afterwards.

## Who hears the recording?

- The research team.
- Hired bilingual assistants who listen and type out what was said. They sign an agreement to keep it confidential.

Nobody else hears it unless you tick an optional box below to allow wider sharing.

## What are the risks?

- Voice cloning: a released recording of your voice could be copied or imitated. We only release audio if you opt in, and we bleep names and identifying details first.
- Being recognised by your voice: if your audio is released publicly, your names are bleeped out but your voice can still be recognised. Public release is optional; by default the audio stays with the team only.
- Personal information: you might say something private about yourself or others. Please avoid discussing sensitive matters. We bleep names and identifying details in any released audio and redact them in transcripts.

We never release voiceprints (the numerical "fingerprint" of a voice).

## When people in a session choose differently

Everyone's voice is picked up on the room microphone and, faintly, on the others' clip-on microphones. So we cannot release one person's voice while hiding another's. Audio from a session is shared or released at a given level only if everyone in that session agreed to that level. If you withdraw, we remove the whole session's audio from the study.

## Your choices (consent)

You tick what you agree to. Tick every box in the first two sections of the consent form to take part. The rest are optional and up to you.

Required:
- take part and be recorded;
- let the research team use the recording for this research.

Optional:
- share with other researchers under a data-use agreement (a signed promise that they use the data only for research and do not pass it on); this sharing includes the audio;
- public release of your audio with names bleeped — your voice can still be recognised;
- public release of transcripts (the written text of what was said) and annotations (markers of who spoke when) only, without the audio;
- be contacted about future studies.

Where we use a few technical words:
- "data-use agreement": a signed promise by other researchers to use the data only for research and not pass it on.
- "annotations": markers we add showing who spoke and when.
- "encrypted": scrambled so only the team, with a key, can open the files.

## Your right to withdraw

You can stop at any time during the session, and withdraw afterwards, without giving a reason, until the results are published (expected [PUBLICATION DATE]). Nothing is shared or released before then, so withdrawing removes you completely. We then remove the whole session's audio from the study and delete the data we made from it.

Anonymised quotes (with names removed) may appear in research papers.

## How is my data kept safe?

Raw audio is encrypted (scrambled so only the team, with a key, can open it) and only the team can open it. It is kept until [RETENTION DATE] and then deleted. Redacted transcripts, with names and identifying details removed, may be kept after that date. See our data management plan for full detail.

You are given a participant code on your take-home copy of the consent form. Quote it if you later want to withdraw or change your choices.

## Legal note

Nepal's Individual Privacy Act, 2075 (2018) says your consent is needed before your personal data is collected, and that it may be used only for the purpose you were told ([overview](https://www.dataguidance.com/opinion/nepal-introduction-individual-privacy-act-2018)). This study follows that. [UNIVERSITY]'s own rules also apply and take precedence.
